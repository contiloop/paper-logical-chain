# Rules for a logical chain

Read by the author and by every reviewer. The layout of the chain is in `prompts/formats/logical-chain.md`.

## What a chain is

A logical chain is the argument of one text, told to its reader, who has not read the text: the problem, the idea, why it should work, what was found, and what follows. The text is a research paper, or another text that argues a point, such as a blog post. The person who asked for the chain will build a talk from it without re-reading the text.

The chain's reader is the one the text is written for. For a research paper that is a researcher of the same field; for another text it is the audience the text addresses, which can be people outside the field. The author names the reader in the chain, and every judgement of what a reader can follow, which terms need explaining and what background a step needs is made for that reader.

The chain speaks for the text. It tells the argument as the text makes it, and it does not report on the text: how the text is written, what it cites, what it leaves undone and where it could be faulted are no part of the talk. The text is the authority: a step is checked against the text, not against the sources the text cites.

The text is in the workspace as `source/<input>/`, with an anchor on every paragraph. So the chain carries the line of the argument and links the rest: a reader who wants a derivation, a setting or a table follows the link.

## Three things that must hold

1. **A listener can follow it.** Each claim comes with what makes it follow from what came before, in a clause or a sentence, and a link to where the text explains it. A person who read earlier chains was stopped where a result came with no reason, where a general claim had no instance, where two statements could not both be held, and where the thing discussed changed with no word on how the two relate.
2. **It claims what the text claims, and no more.** An assumption stays an assumption, and a result of one experiment stays that; the limits the text itself puts on a claim stay with the claim. What a step says of a figure or a table is true of it: look at it first.
3. **It holds what the talk needs, and nothing else.** The author first writes the spine: the text's conclusion, and under it, in order, the claims that conclusion rests on. A step is a link of the spine. Whatever can be removed with the way to the conclusion still standing, and the talk no different, is removed.

These are judged as the person giving the talk would judge them, not one sentence at a time against a list.

## Language

The chain is in the language the person asked for, and in the language of the text when they asked for none, written as a writer of that language writes.
